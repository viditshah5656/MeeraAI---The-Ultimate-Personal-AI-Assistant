## 📋 Executive Summary & Theoretical Ontology

### 1. Formal Definition & Scope
Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) represents the theoretical and operational limit where an artificial system possesses cross-domain cognitive autonomy, sample-efficient zero-shot adaptation, counterfactual reasoning, and self-directed goal synthesis at or exceeding median human capacities. 

Formally, let $\mathcal{E}$ denote the space of all computable, bounded environments governed by an unknown probability distribution $\mu \in \mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E})$, and let $\mathcal{T}$ denote a rich taxonomy of goals. A system $A$ achieves AGI if its expected performance measure $V_A$ across all environments $e \in \mathcal{E}$ under computational budget constraints $\mathcal{C}$ satisfies:

$$V_A = \mathbb{E}_{\mu(e), \tau \sim \mathcal{T}} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t r_t^{(e, \tau)} \right] \ge \mathbb{E}_{\text{human}} \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{\infty} \gamma^t r_t^{(e, \tau)} \right] - \epsilon$$

where $\gamma \in (0, 1)$ is the discount factor, $r_t$ is the scalar environment feedback reward, and $\epsilon \ge 0$ is an arbitrarily small domain-generality tolerance parameter.

## 📐 Mathematical Foundations & Formal Derivations

### 1. Information-Theoretic Lower Bounds on Generalization
Consider a data generating distribution $P(X, Y)$. Pre-training maximizes the mutual information $I(X; Z)$ between the raw sensory/text inputs $X$ and the latent representation $Z$, while minimizing the empirical risk over $N$ tokens. By the **Information Bottleneck Principle** ([Tishby et al., 2000](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0004057)), the optimal representation $Z$ minimizes the Lagrangian:

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{IB}}[P(Z|X)] = I(X; Z) - \beta I(Z; Y)$$

For an AGI system to generalize to an unseen domain $\mathcal{D}_{\text{out}}$ with bounded sample complexity $M$, the generalization error $\epsilon_{\text{gen}} = |\mathcal{R}_{\text{emp}} - \mathcal{R}_{\text{true}}|$ is bounded by the cross-entropy difference and the total variation distance $D_{\text{TV}}$ between the source distribution $\mathcal{P}_{\text{src}}$ and target distribution $\mathcal{P}_{\text{tgt}}$:

$$\epsilon_{\text{gen}} \le \sqrt{\frac{2 K_{\text{VC}}}{M} \log \left( \frac{2eM}{K_{\text{VC}}} \right)} + 2 \, D_{\text{TV}}(\mathcal{P}_{\text{src}} \,||\, \mathcal{P}_{\text{tgt}})$$

where $K_{\text{VC}}$ represents the Vapnik-Chervonenkis dimension of the model class.

### 2. Derivation of Pre-training Power-Law Scaling Limits
Empirical observations by [Kaplan et al. (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361) and refined by [Chinchilla / Hoffmann et al. (2022)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556) show that cross-entropy loss $L(N, D)$ decomposes into a power-law relationship parameterized by model parameters $N$ and data tokens $D$:

$$L(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^\alpha} + \frac{B}{D^\beta}$$

where $E$ is the irreducible entropy of the data distribution, $A, B$ are scale constants, and $\alpha, \beta$ are power-law exponents.

#### Derivation of Compute-Optimal Allocation
Let $C \approx 6ND$ be the floating-point operations (FLOPs) required for training a Transformer network. To find the optimal allocation $(N^*, D^*)$ that minimizes $L(N, D)$ under a fixed compute budget $C_0$:

$$\min_{N, D} \left( E + A N^{-\alpha} + B D^{-\beta} \right) \quad \text{s.t.} \quad 6ND = C_0$$

Forming the Lagrangian multiplier equation:

$$\mathcal{L}(N, D, \lambda) = E + A N^{-\alpha} + B D^{-\beta} + \lambda (6ND - C_0)$$

Taking partial derivatives with respect to $N$ and $D$, and setting them to zero:

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial N} = -\alpha A N^{-\alpha - 1} + 6 \lambda D = 0 \implies 6 \lambda D = \alpha A N^{-\alpha - 1}$$

$$\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial D} = -\beta B D^{-\beta - 1} + 6 \lambda N = 0 \implies 6 \lambda N = \beta B D^{-\beta - 1}$$

Multiplying the first equation by $N$ and the second by $D$:

$$6 \lambda N D = \alpha A N^{-\alpha} = \beta B D^{-\beta}$$

Equating the two terms yields:

$$\alpha A N^{-\alpha} = \beta B D^{-\beta} \implies D = \left( \frac{\beta B}{\alpha A} \right)^{\frac{1}{\beta}} N^{\frac{\alpha}{\beta}}$$

Substituting $D = \frac{C_0}{6N}$ into the equality:

$$\alpha A N^{-\alpha} = \beta B \left( \frac{C_0}{6N} \right)^{-\beta} = \beta B \left( \frac{6}{C_0} \right)^\beta N^\beta$$

Solving for $N$:

$$N^{\alpha + \beta} = \frac{\alpha A}{\beta B} \left( \frac{C_0}{6} \right)^\beta \implies N^* \propto C_0^{\frac{\beta}{\alpha + \beta}}$$

By symmetry:

$$D^* \propto C_0^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha + \beta}}$$

When $\alpha \approx \beta \approx 0.5$ (as derived empirically in [Hoffmann et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556)), $N^* \propto C_0^{0.5}$ and $D^* \propto C_0^{0.5}$, proving that parameters and token counts must scale in equal proportions.

### 3. Inference-Time Compute Scaling & System 2 Search Equations
Modern reasoning models (e.g., OpenAI o1/o3, DeepSeek-R1) shift compute scaling from pre-training to test-time search over process trees via Reinforcement Learning (RL).

Let $T_{test}$ be the test-time compute allocation budget. The target objective is to maximize the expected verification score $R(S)$ over a search tree generated by a policy $\pi_\theta(a|s)$:

$$\max_{\pi_\theta, T_{\text{test}}} \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim \pi_\theta} \left[ R(\tau) \;\Big|\; \text{Cost}(\tau) \le T_{\text{test}} \right]$$

#### Tree-Search Value Optimization
Under Process-Supervised Reward Models (PRMs) combined with Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS), the action-value function $Q(s, a)$ for expanding node $s$ with action $a$ is updated according to:

$$Q(s, a) = (1 - \eta) Q(s, a) + \eta \left( r_{\text{step}}(s, a) + \gamma \max_{a'} Q(s', a') \right)$$

The upper confidence bound for trees (UCT) selection policy is:

$$a^* = \arg\max_a \left( Q(s, a) + c_{\text{puct}} \cdot P(a|s) \cdot \frac{\sqrt{\sum_{b} N(s, b)}}{1 + N(s, a)} \right)$$

The test-time compute scaling law ([Snell et al., 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314)) establishes that test-time search can compensate for model parameter scale by:

$$\text{Accuracy}(T_{\text{test}}) \approx 1 - \exp\left( - \lambda \cdot N^{\gamma_1} \cdot T_{\text{test}}^{\gamma_2} \right)$$

where $\gamma_2 > 0$ denotes the efficiency scaling of tree exploration under precise verifier guidance.

## 💻 Algorithmic Formulations & Reference Implementation

Below is a self-contained PyTorch reference implementation of **Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)** combined with a **Process-Guided Inference Search Engine**, a foundational algorithmic mechanism underlying modern System 2 reasoning models (e.g., [DeepSeek-AI et al. (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948)).

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn
import torch.nn.functional as F
from typing import List, Dict, Tuple

class ProcessRewardModel(nn.Module):
    """
    Step-wise Process Reward Model (PRM) that evaluates intermediate reasoning steps.
    """
    def __init__(self, d_model: int):
        super().__init__()
        self.step_score_head = nn.Linear(d_model, 1)

    def forward(self, hidden_states: torch.Tensor, step_mask: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        """
        hidden_states: [Batch, Seq_Len, d_model]
        step_mask: [Batch, Seq_Len] (1 at step termination tokens, 0 elsewhere)
        Returns: [Batch, Num_Steps] step scores
        """
        logits = self.step_score_head(hidden_states).squeeze(-1)  # [Batch, Seq_Len]
        step_scores = logits * step_mask
        return step_scores

class GRPOLoss(nn.Module):
    """
    Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) Loss Function.
    Eliminates the value network by computing relative advantage within a group of 
    sampled outputs for a single prompt.
    """
    def __init__(self, clip_eps: float = 0.2, kl_coeff: float = 0.01):
        super().__init__()
        self.clip_eps = clip_eps
        self.kl_coeff = kl_coeff

    def forward(
        self,
        log_probs: torch.Tensor,         # [Group_Size, Seq_Len]
        old_log_probs: torch.Tensor,     # [Group_Size, Seq_Len]
        ref_log_probs: torch.Tensor,     # [Group_Size, Seq_Len]
        rewards: torch.Tensor,           # [Group_Size]
        mask: torch.Tensor               # [Group_Size, Seq_Len]
    ) -> torch.Tensor:
        # Normalize rewards across the sampled group (Group Relative Advantage)
        mean_reward = rewards.mean()
        std_reward = rewards.std() + 1e-8
        advantages = (rewards - mean_reward) / std_reward  # [Group_Size]
        advantages = advantages.unsqueeze(-1)              # [Group_Size, 1]

        # Probability ratio
        ratio = torch.exp(log_probs - old_log_probs)

        # Clipped Surrogate Objective
        surr1 = ratio * advantages
        surr2 = torch.clamp(ratio, 1.0 - self.clip_eps, 1.0 + self.clip_eps) * advantages
        policy_loss = -torch.min(surr1, surr2)

        # KL Divergence Penalty w.r.t Reference Policy
        kl_div = torch.exp(ref_log_probs - log_probs) - (ref_log_probs - log_probs) - 1
        
        total_loss = (policy_loss + self.kl_coeff * kl_div) * mask
        return total_loss.sum() / mask.sum()

class System2SearchNode:
    """
    Node representation for test-time tree search driven by PRM scoring.
    """
    def __init__(self, state_text: str, parent=None, prior_p: float = 1.0):
        self.state_text = state_text
        self.parent = parent
        self.children: List['System2SearchNode'] = []
        self.visit_count: int = 0
        self.value_sum: float = 0.0
        self.prior_p: float = prior_p

    @property
    def Q(self) -> float:
        return self.value_sum / self.visit_count if self.visit_count > 0 else 0.0

def uct_select(node: System2SearchNode, c_puct: float = 1.414) -> System2SearchNode:
    """Selects child node maximizing Upper Confidence Bound for Trees."""
    total_visits = sum(child.visit_count for child in node.children)
    
    def puct_score(child: System2SearchNode) -> float:
        u = c_puct * child.prior_p * (total_visits ** 0.5) / (1 + child.visit_count)
        return child.Q + u

    return max(node.children, key=puct_score)
```

### 1. The Auto-regressive Entropy Trap & Halting Problem
Auto-regressive language modeling generates sequences via step-by-step factorization:

$$P(X_1, \dots, X_T) = \prod_{t=1}^T P(X_t \mid X_1, \dots, X_{t-1})$$

Because errors compound exponentially with generation length $T$, the probability of a trajectory remaining error-free decays as:

$$P_{\text{correct}}(T) \approx (1 - \epsilon)^T \xrightarrow[T \to \infty]{} 0$$

Without a external verifier runtime or continuous test-time state estimation, raw auto-regressive generation fails over ultra-long horizon tasks. Furthermore, deciding whether a reasoning sequence will reach a correct conclusion or loop indefinitely is mapping directly to the **Turing Halting Problem**, placing absolute logical bounds on unguided test-time search.

### 2. Data Wall and the Synthetic Collapse Limit
High-quality human-generated text on the public internet ($\sim 10^{14}$ tokens) is largely exhausted. Relying on synthetic data without strict verification leads to **Model Collapse** ([Shumailov et al., 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07566-y)). If a model is trained recursively on its own output distribution:

$$Q_{n+1} = \mathcal{M}(Q_n)$$

the variance of the data distribution vanishes over time, leading to representation degradation and loss of functional tail generalization.

### 3. Causal Discovery and Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Barriers
Current architectures excel at statistical correlation within the training distribution (Judea Pearl’s **Level 1: Association**). However, true general intelligence requires **Level 2: Intervention** ($P(Y | \text{do}(X))$) and **Level 3: Counterfactuals** ($P(Y_{X=x} | X=x', Y=y')$). Transformers do not natively represent structural causal models (SCMs), making true zero-shot out-of-distribution reasoning fragile.

### 1. Joint-Embedding Predictive Architectures (JEPA) for Physical Grounding
Abandon pixel/token reconstruction in favor of latent space state prediction ([LeCun, 2022](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-fnb)). By predicting abstract latent trajectories rather than detailed concrete outputs, agents can plan over long time horizons without accumulating micro-level errors:

$$\min_{\theta, \phi} \mathcal{D}\left( s_{t+1}, \text{Predictor}_\theta(\text{Encoder}_\phi(x_t), a_t) \right)$$

### 2. Neuro-Symbolic Verification Engine Integration
System 2 search must be anchored in deterministic logic engines. Future architectures will route reasoning steps directly to formal verifiers (Lean 4, Z3 SMT solvers, executable code sandboxes), substituting probabilistic reward models with **exact logical verification**.

### 3. Test-Time Adaptive Meta-Learning (Plasticity)
To solve sample inefficiency, architectures must transition from frozen post-training parameters to **continuous in-weights updating**. Utilizing gradient-based meta-learning (e.g., MAML-style inner-loop adaptation), the model modifies its key-value parameters dynamically during inference, achieving human-like rapid adaptation to novel environments.
