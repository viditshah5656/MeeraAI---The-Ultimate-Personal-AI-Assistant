### 💡 Practical Travel Tips

1. **Visas:** Most travelers are eligible for a **Visa on Arrival (VOA)** valid for 30 days, which can be extended once for an additional 30 days.
2. **Transportation:** 
   * Renting a scooter is popular but recommended only if experienced, due to heavy traffic.
   * Ride-hailing apps like **Gojek** and **Grab** work well in major towns (Canggu, Seminyak, Ubud, South Bali), though local taxi zones exist in certain areas. Hiring a private driver for full-day trips ($35–$50 USD/day) is highly cost-effective and convenient.
3. **Currency & Payments:** Indonesian Rupiah (IDR). Credit cards are widely accepted in cafes, hotels, and upscale restaurants, but carrying cash is essential for local markets, entrance fees, and small warungs (local eateries).
4. **Cultural Etiquette:** Cover shoulders and knees when visiting temples (sarongs are usually available for rent or provided at entrances). Never step directly on *Canang Sari* (daily palm-leaf offerings placed on sidewalks).

### ✈️ Practical Travel Essentials

#### **Best Time to Visit**
* **Dry Season (April – October):** Best for outdoor activities, hiking, beach days, and low humidity. July and August are peak tourist months.
* **Wet Season (November – March):** Higher humidity and frequent tropical downpours (usually short-lived). Lower hotel rates and fewer crowds.

#### **Transportation & Logistics**
* **Airports:** Denpasar International Airport (DPS) is the sole international gateway.
* **Getting Around:** Renting a scooter is popular for short distances in Canggu or Ubud (wear a helmet and carry an International Driving Permit). For longer inter-regional transfers, hire a private car with driver via ride-hailing apps like **Grab** or **Gojek**.
* **Fast Boats:** Daily speedboats run from Sanur or Padang Bai port to Nusa Penida, Nusa Lembongan, and the Gili Islands.
