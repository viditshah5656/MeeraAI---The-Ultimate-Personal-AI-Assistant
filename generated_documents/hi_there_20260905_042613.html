🛑 Critical Bottlenecks & Theoretical Limits

1. Thermodynamic & Hardware Memory Walls

The dominant barrier in scaling transformer architectures during inference is memory bandwidth, driven by the size of the Key-Value (KV) cache.

Derivation of KV-Cache Memory Scaling and Bandwidth Ceiling

Let B be the batch size, L be the sequence length (context length), n_l be the number of transformer layers, h_k be the number of key-value heads, and d_k be the dimension per head. The total bytes required to store the KV cache in precision P (in bytes per element, e.g., P = 2 for FP16) is derived as:

\text{Memory}_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times B \times L \times n_l \times h_k \times d_k \times P

For a 175B model where n_l = 96, h_k = 8 (Grouped-Query Attention with 8 KV groups), d_k = 128, operating at B = 32, L = 128,000 tokens, under FP16 (P = 2):

\text{Memory}_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times 32 \times 128,000 \times 96 \times 8 \times 128 \times 2 \text{ bytes}
\text{Memory}_{\text{KV}} = 2 \times 32 \times 128,000 \times 96 \times 1,024 \times 2 = 1,610,612,736,000 \text{ bytes} \approx 1.61 \text{ TB}

This demonstrates that context sizes scaling to 100\text{k}+ tokens shift operational dynamics from compute-bound matrix multiplications to memory-bandwidth-bound cache transfers, hitting the "Memory Wall."

2. Theoretical Scaling Laws & Data Scarcity

According to empirical compute-optimal scaling laws (Kaplan et al. (2020), Hoffmann et al. (2022)), the loss L(N, D) as a function of parameters N and token count D follows a power-law relationship:

L(N, D) = E + \frac{A}{N^\alpha} + \frac{B}{D^\beta}

Where E is the irreducible loss, A and B are constants, and \alpha \approx 0.34, \beta \approx 0.28. Equal scaling of compute allocation requires N \propto C^{a} and D \propto C^{b} where a + b = 1 and a \approx 0.5, b \approx 0.5.

3. Causal Reasoning Barriers & Unseen Autoregressive Fallacies

Autoregressive models trained via Next-Token Prediction minimize empirical risk over P(x_t \mid x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}). However, this formulation struggles with structural causal inference.

Let G = (V, E) be a causal Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) over random variables V. A model estimating conditional probabilities P(Y \mid X) fails to compute interventional probabilities P(Y \mid \text{do}(X)) without structural causal priors, leading to errors in formal logical deduction, dynamic environment planning, and inverse constraint satisfaction.
